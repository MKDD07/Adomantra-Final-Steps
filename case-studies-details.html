How Adomantra Took 10on10 Foods from ₹12 Lakhs to ₹58 Lakhs/Month on Quick Commerce

  2. Case Studies

Marketplace Optimization Case Study 12-Week Campaign

Summary

10on10 Foods, a ₹180 crore FMCG brand, had 38 SKUs listed across quick commerce platforms but was generating only ₹12 lakhs/month after 8 weeks — severely underperforming. Buried on Page 5–6 of search results, with 22% out-of-stock incidents and no quick commerce–specific listing strategy, the brand was invisible. Adomantra deployed its Marketplace Optimization Framework: competitive SKU audit, quick commerce–native listing rewrites, pincode-level inventory coordination, and pricing A/B tests. In 12 weeks, monthly revenue surged to ₹58 lakhs (+383%), average search rank reached Position 2–3, repeat purchase rate climbed to 41% by Week 4, and out-of-stock incidents dropped to just 3%.

The Project

A focused Marketplace Optimization engagement for 10on10 Foods — a ₹180 crore FMCG brand — to establish a high-performing quick commerce presence across India's leading platforms.

The Objective

To audit and restructure 10on10 Foods' quick commerce listings, identify the highest-velocity SKUs, and deploy pincode-wise inventory coordination to unlock search visibility and drive consistent revenue.

The Ultimate Result

Monthly quick commerce revenue grew from ₹12 lakhs to ₹58 lakhs (+383%) in just 12 weeks, with average search rank at Position 2–3 and repeat purchase rate reaching 41% by Week 4.

THE CORE CHALLENGE: WHY WAS 10ON10 FOODS INVISIBLE?

Despite having 38 SKUs listed across major quick commerce platforms, 10on10 Foods was generating just ₹12 lakhs per month after 8 weeks of being live — far below category benchmarks. Three structural problems were holding the brand back.

The Three Critical Failure Points:

  • Listing Invisibility: Core products like 'high-protein atta' and 'jowar flour' ranked on Page 5–6 of search results. Quick commerce algorithms favor high-speed SKUs (>100 units/day) and inventory availability signals — new, unoptimized listings get buried instantly.
  • Content Overhaul: 10on10 Foods was using traditional e-commerce titles like '₹299 High Protein Atta 1kg Rich in Protein' — far exceeding the 45–50 character quick commerce limit and ignoring the platform's 1–2 word search behavior patterns.
  • Inventory Fragmentation: With a 22% daily out-of-stock rate and no pincode-level demand tracking, stock mismatches were causing geographic inconsistencies and killing repeat purchase intent.

When 10on10 Foods first came to Adomantra, the brand was practically invisible on quick commerce platforms despite having a strong product portfolio and an established name in the FMCG space. Their listings were buried deep in search results, inventory was consistently running out across key pincodes, and competitor brands were dominating every relevant search term. Customers searching for everyday staples like atta and flour were finding other brands first—not because 10on10's products were inferior, but because the brand had never learned the rules of this new game.

The core problem was that they had approached quick commerce the same way they approached traditional e-commerce—copying listings across, proposing an overwhelming number of SKUs, and hoping the algorithm would figure it out. That approach does not work on quick commerce, where the algorithm rewards speed, availability, and hyper-local relevance above everything else.
To understand the scale of what 10on10 was up against, you need to understand how quick commerce actually works. On platforms like Blinkit, Zepto, and Instamart, a customer does not scroll. They do not browse through pages of results the way they might on Amazon or Flipkart. They type what they need—"atta," "whole wheat flour," "besan"—and they tap one of the first three results. Often, they tap the very first one. The entire decision happens in under eight seconds. If your product is not visible in that window, you do not exist. There is no second page. There is no "customers also bought." There is no loyalty to a brand the customer has never seen. The algorithm decides who lives and who disappears, and it makes that decision based on a handful of variables: stock availability across dark stores, order velocity, listing quality, pricing competitiveness, and delivery speed promise. Miss even one of those, and your ranking collapses.

Why Delay Hurts

Your Journey

Adomantra's first move was to completely rethink the portfolio strategy. Rather than trying to compete across hundreds of products simultaneously, the team identified the small set of SKUs that had the highest potential to gain traction quickly — products that matched how urban consumers actually search and shop on these platforms. Quick commerce users do not browse the way they do on traditional e-commerce sites. They type one or two words, they want it fast, and they make decisions in seconds. Listings were rebuilt from scratch to match this behavior — titles rewritten within the tight character limits the platform imposes, images replaced with clean packaging shots that dark store delivery agents could identify instantly without confusion, and every pricing decision carefully tested across multiple store locations to find the exact points where conversions happened most efficiently. Nothing was assumed. Everything was tested.

Strategic Takeaways from the 10on10 Foods Case

Less Is More on Quick Commerce:

6 optimized SKUs outperformed 38 unoptimized listings. Focus on velocity over volume.

Title Format Is Non-Negotiable:

Quick commerce titles need to be 45–50 characters, keyword-first, and thumb-stop clear — traditional formats actively hurt discoverability.

Inventory Is a Marketing Asset:

Pincode-level stock management eliminated geographic gaps and turned availability into a competitive advantage.

On the supply side, the work was equally detailed. Adomantra coordinated directly with dark store managers to understand demand patterns at the pincode level — because what sells heavily in one part of a city may move slowly in another, and a brand that treats its entire city as one market will always have stock in the wrong places. Automated alerts were set up so that whenever inventory dropped to a critical threshold, the supply chain was notified immediately, preventing the stockouts that had been quietly killing the brand's algorithmic ranking. As availability improved, the algorithm responded — rankings climbed, more customers discovered the products, and the cycle reinforced itself. By the end of the engagement, the brand had gone from near-invisible search rankings to consistently appearing among the top results for its core categories, stockouts had become a rare exception rather than a weekly crisis, customers who ordered once were coming back in large numbers within the same month, and average order values had grown meaningfully as shoppers began adding more to their baskets with each visit.
